# Supplement — Methodological details

Supplement to the article "Explicit realisation of the 'parameter ↔ unstable manifold' correspondence for period-doubling universality classes"; section numbering S1…S9; each section indicates to which section of the main text it relates.

## S1. Numerical pipeline pitfall traps (= M1; relates to §5)

Five systematic errors of the pipeline, recorded as a warning for reproducibility: (1) the original d = 2 catalogue stores the position of the cascade at level m = 7 exactly, not the extrapolated limit — treated by Richardson's method before being used as an anchor; (2) circularity of the cascade tail (§5.4) — the anchor constant a_F, built by Richardson from the upper levels of the same cascade, makes these levels identically circular for the fit using the same constant (signature — coincidence of the correction ratio with δ to 11 significant digits); (3) an error ε of the anchor constant injects a growing, not a decaying parasitic term A·ε·δ^m (§5.4; for d = 4 the value ε ≈ 8.6×10⁻²² at the claimed 4×10⁻²³ explained the outlier of the ratio); (4) the small-step criterion of Newton lies on chaotic orbits (the step may become vanishingly small while the actual residual |x_P| is large) — a direct check of the orbit residual is mandatory; (5) the cosine between the eigenvectors of the discretized operator cannot be taken on the coefficient vectors in the monomial basis {t^j} at N ≳ 48: the basis is poorly conditioned, and the coefficient cosine of two nearly coinciding functions may be 0.05–0.7 (source of the apparent "asymmetry d = 4 / d = 6" — a basis-dependent metric artefact, §2.5) — one must compare values of the functions on a common grid or coefficients in the Chebyshev basis.

## S2. Spectral hunt (= M2; relates to §2.3, §2.4)

Deflated power iteration (isolate the leading eigenvalue, subtract the contribution, repeat) breaks down for d = 2 after 2–3 levels and for d = 6 from the very start (strong non-normality of the linearization matrix). The working route: full diagonalization (mpmath.eig) or a ladder in N with shifted inverse iteration and controlled functional residual on a dense grid. By this route the sign of λ₃(4) was corrected (§2.3) and the simplicity of α^(−2) at d = 2 was confirmed (§2.4).

## S3. Slow series and bootstrap on flat maps (= M3 + M4; relates to §6.3)

The tail of the H_d expansion at N = 48 is of order 10⁻¹⁷ for d = 4, but only 3×10⁻⁶ for d = 6; the series cutoff window is calibrated on the actual tail of the class, not inherited. For large d (≈ d ≥ 13) Newton's method starting from the previous cascade level on flat maps systematically fails to converge; the solution — scan plus bisection by the sign of x_{2^k}(a), not applied in one of the sweep runs (skip of the node d = 8 in T6.1, §6.3).

## S4. Tamper testing (= M5; relates to §4)

For both Krawczyk certification runs and for "Bridge"/"Bridge-2" the battery of deliberate corruptions of the input and finished certificates is applied (rebuilding a corrupted input with a generator; direct forgery of the certificate file fields), internally consistent, not detected by syntactic check: 14/14 (d = 4), 16/16 (d = 6, including types for the two-layer seed structure), 8/8 (first attempt at "Bridge"), 24/24 (8 types × 3 points, "Bridge-2"); the positive controls are clean.

## S5. Lattice forensics of step 1/16 (d = 4 and d = 6; relates to §3.3, §7.2)

**Definition of "near a node" (verbatim, B.6.1).** frac16(x) = min((16x) mod 1, 1 − (16x) mod 1); a point is "near a node" ⇔ frac16 < 1/16, i.e. |x − k/16| < 1/256 for some integer k (per coordinate separately); the null model is uniform: 12.5 % per coordinate, 1.5625 % joint.

The catalogue positions of the nine holdouts lie unusually close to the nodes of the 1/16 lattice in the u-plane; a check on the full d = 4, d = 6 catalogues showed that the proximity to nodes is a property of the catalogues as a whole (over the full catalogues: per-coordinate 36.1–52.9 %, 2D joint 22.9–39.2 % vs. background 1.56 %, p ≪ 10⁻¹⁵; in the ORIG-ONLY subset — zeros lost by the rescan — noticeably higher, up to 69.0 % joint). The decisive test — a rescan with an incommensurate step 1/24 and a random shift (the same pipeline, without level pairing), the same protocol for both classes.

*d = 4:* |orig| = 62, |new| = 146 (zeros of level 8), |orig ∩ new| = 31 (= BOTH); ORIG-ONLY = 62 − 31 = 31 (lost by the rescan), NEW-ONLY = 146 − 31 = 115 (found only by the rescan). The fraction near nodes (joint): ORIG-ONLY — 41.9 % (p = 5·10⁻¹⁶, maximum); BOTH — 6.5 % (p = 0.084, not significant); NEW-ONLY — 0.0 %. The "shifted 1/16 grid" control (74 zeros): they do not cling to the original nodes (joint 2.7 %, p = 0.32), but strongly to their shifted ones (31.1 %, p = 1·10⁻²³). Mechanism: all 31 ORIG-ONLY are lost at the candidate selection stage (28/31 — the node is not a local minimum of |H|, 0.2–3.7, not a "valley"; 3/31 — invalid by domain_check); Newton from a valid candidate always converges to a zero.

*d = 6:* 641 zeros of level 8 vs. 342 original; matching BOTH = 155, ORIG-ONLY = 187, NEW-ONLY = 486 (`r1b_match.py` → `r1b_match.json`, REPORT-R1-LATTICE-RESCAN.md, §"0. Matching"); the exact rule for matching BOTH pairs between level 8 (rescan) and the level-9 catalogue is not documented in the README/REPORT of this rescan (only the script and the resulting counters are given). The fraction near nodes (joint16): ORIG — 39.2 % (p = 4.5·10⁻¹⁴⁶); ORIG-ONLY — 69.0 % (Re 78.1 %, Im 80.2 %; p = 5.0·10⁻¹⁸⁵, maximum); BOTH — 3.2 % (p = 0.097, borderline); NEW-ONLY — 0.0 % (null model 1.56 %). Reasons for the loss of all 187 ORIG-ONLY (the full set, not a sample): 86.1 % — the node is not a local minimum of |H|, 12.8 % — invalid by domain_check, 1.1 % — Newton converged to another zero; the same candidate-selection mechanism as for d = 4. The level-m = 7 partner — a binary separator: 100 % for BOTH/ORIG-ONLY, 0/60 in the NEW-ONLY sample. Authenticity (independent check |H₈| < 10⁻¹⁵): 100 % in all samples (60 ORIG-ONLY, 60 NEW-ONLY, all 155 BOTH) — including the new zeros, they are genuine roots of H₆, not optimizer junk; the Newton-return test from a 1e-3 perturbation for d = 6 is uninformative (passes only 20–28 % even for doubly confirmed BOTH — narrow basins of attraction of degree 6, not a sign of forgery). The nine "late" zeros — an extreme case: maximum "lattice-nicity" in the catalogue (100 % near a q = 16 node), but not a single one was recovered by the rescan (0/9) — a set rejected by the standard pipeline and found only by a separate method (interval Krawczyk at m = 10–12, §4.3), not reproduced by the rescan.

**Conclusion for both classes: a grid-selection artefact**, established by the same protocol (proved for d = 4 and d = 6). The "valley" of small |H| for many zeros is already of the grid step, so a zero enters the catalogue if and only if a grid node happened to fall in its valley — this is the observed "lattice".

In addition: the new zeros in both classes are confirmed as genuine (|H₈| < 10⁻¹⁵ independently; numbers of catalogue incompleteness — §3.2) — density/specificity statistics are computed on an incomplete sample (§3.4, §7.4).

For the class d = 2 the lattice is present but weaker: per-coordinate ≈ 30.2 % / 27.4 %, joint ≈ 12.9 % (441, with conjugates); an independent recomputation on 222 without conjugates — **13.51 %** (30/222; Appendix B.6.1, row `unique222`; the earlier ≈24 % is not reproduced). The control sub-catalogue known23 (23 certified zeros, entering the correspondence of §3.2) does NOT show a lattice: joint 1/23 (p = 0.30) — independent confirmation of the selection-artefact picture: a curated catalogue, not built by the same coarse scan, does not exhibit a lattice. The mechanism is the same for all three classes: proximity to the nodes of the construction grid, not orbital combinatorics.

## S6. Layer 2 — S078 and S090: fragility and pow/mul cross-check (relates to §4.3)

The storage format of d = 6 seeds (imaginary part — float64, not an exact string) for most parameters is immaterial (residual ≤ 2.0×10⁻¹²), but for S078, S090 — points with extreme |F′(a*)| ≈ 8.1×10¹⁴ and 1.1×10¹⁸ (the most poorly conditioned in the catalogue) — it is insufficient: a box around the stored seed already fails to be certified at radius 10⁻²⁵ (S078) / 10⁻²⁹ (S090) — estimate BEFORE the recheck; the recheck itself REPORT-R2-S090 started from other, narrower radii 1e-30/1e-31 (the next narrowing step, the numbers do not contradict each other). Solution: deterministic refinement — eight Newton iterations at dps = 120, truncation of the centre to 60 significant digits; identity with the root of the blind run is locked by a pin of the distance |c_refined − seed|_upper = 1.74×10⁻²⁴ (S078) / 1.85×10⁻²⁴ (S090), both < 5×10⁻²⁴ (the nearest other box — 2.6×10⁻¹⁰).

The independent recheck (REPORT-R2-S090) revealed the fragility of the original layer-2 radius (r = 10⁻³¹ for S090) to the order of computing the sixth power: in the author's order (x⁶ = x**6) the ball F′(X) does not contain 0 (relative width |F′(X)| ≈ 6.8 %), but in the mathematically equivalent order x⁶ = x⁵·x — it does, and the certificate in this order fails. Reducing the radius to r = 10⁻³⁴ (prec = 512) removes the fragility: ball width |F′(X)| = 1.36×10⁻³ (mul) / 6.45×10⁻⁵ (pow) for S090, ≤ 1.14×10⁻⁷ for S078, in both orders and at prec 256/512/1024. Final layer-2 radii in Appendix B: 1×10⁻³⁴ for S078 and S090. **Coverage of the pow/mul cross-check:** systematically run on five boxes — S078, S090 (layer 2) and three layer-1 controls (S001, S172 — worst isolation, S180 — maximum admissible κ), not on all 287; for the remaining 282 boxes the independent second code path (T4.1-b) confirms the result only in one (the author's) order of exponentiation.

## S7. "Bridge-2" — detailed walk-through (relates to §4.4)

**λ₂(2).** A certified eigenpair of the N = 48 collocation; the reduction to the limiting operator (in the spirit of Lanford / Eckmann–Wittwer) requires hypotheses that are not checked here. Result: λ₂(2) = −0.1236527125526870243353936525855 ± 1.82×10⁻³³ (discretization N = 48) — status **numerically verified** (§2.3), not "RIGOROUS" for the limiting operator; no full certification of the operator.

**λ₂(4), λ₂(6) — "Bridge-2" (Chebyshev basis).** The first attempt (monomial basis) gave rigorous certificates for N = 16, 24, but with a gap of 18–30 orders between the certified centre and the T2.1 float-anchor — a consequence of the ~1000× growth of the Jacobian condition number per +4 of N (strong non-normality of the operator in the monomial basis). The transfer to the Chebyshev basis (G(t) = Σ b_k T_k(2t−1), Gauss–Chebyshev collocation, the same chain Krawczyk, stages K1/K2) reduces the growth of the condition number to polynomial (~7·10⁵ at N = 16 to ~1.5·10⁸ at N = 48); K(X) ⊂ int(X) passes on the whole ladder N = 16, 24, 32, 40 (d = 4) and up to 48 (d = 6), ball radii of λ₂ ~10⁻⁴⁰…10⁻⁴¹.

Certified centres: λ₂(4) = 0.291838408983545104530996961… (N = 40), λ₂(6) = 0.371071467681930132470983381… (N = 48). The truncation error along the ladder decreases in accelerated fashion: d = 4 — 4.2·10⁻¹² → 2.5·10⁻¹⁸ → 3.5·10⁻²⁶ (16→24→32→40); d = 6 — 2.1·10⁻⁸ → 3.0·10⁻¹⁴ → 3.8·10⁻²⁰ → 2.0·10⁻²⁶ (16→…→48). Both float-anchors of T2.1 are reproduced by the certified centres in all 20 significant digits.

**Three quantities at N = 48 (d = 6), explicitly distinguished.** `trunc_vs_prev = 1.972×10⁻²⁶` — |λ₂^{(48)} − λ₂^{(40)}|, truncation error of the Bridge-2 ladder. `anchor_diff = 9.834×10⁻²²` — |λ₂(N) − anchor_λ₂|, distance to the pilot anchor; stagnation from N = 40 means that this is the precision of the anchor itself, not of the certificate. A separate non-certified check: an independent mpmath-Newton continuation up to N = 44 reproduces the anchor with precision 1.88×10⁻²⁶ (d = 6) and 9.97×10⁻²² (d = 4) — a third, independent path.

**Status (§2.3):** **numerically verified** — the certificate proves existence and uniqueness of an eigenvalue of the collocation system of the stated N in the ball, not of the limiting operator; a rigorous N → ∞ tail estimate is beyond the scope of the work. The "Bridge-2" tamper battery — 24/24 (Supplement §S4); the second code path (dps 90) confirms residuals ~10⁻⁶⁹ and coincidence of λ₂ to 64–65 digits.

## S8. OOS / LOOCV procedures (relates to §7.3)

The model of the correspondence residual is two-term: e(m) ~ −(C·δ^(−m) + D·λ₂^m); the observed behaviour — that the slower of the two terms dominates.

- **Direct detection of the λ₂ mode under the δ layer at d = 2 (observation awaiting independent reproduction).** After subtracting the δ layer, the remainder is sign-alternating (mandatory, since λ₂(2) < 0): 5 sign alternations out of 6 possible, with the ratio of successive remainders 7.88–7.89 against the predicted 1/|λ₂(2)| = 8.087. The λ₂ mode under the δ layer at d = 2 — NOT RIGOROUS (see the Dependency map): the specific m levels at which this observation is recorded have not been re-confirmed by a separate reproduction with explicit pinning of m. The leave-one-out cross-check shows an improvement of the two-term model over the one-term one by a factor of 58 (calibration data) and by 300–530 (real-zeros data of H).
- **Blind confirmation at d = 6.** The d = 6 class is computed by a frozen procedure that picks the best convergence rate from eight candidates, unaware of the law min(δ, 1/|λ₂|) at the time of the run. The protocol itself picked 1/λ₂(6) as the best fit (geometric mean deviation −0.2 %, the closest competitor — +8.7 %).
- **The d = 6 interference explained.** The spread of the observed ratios at d = 6 (0.6…7.5 depending on the m range and pair of parameters) is a consequence of the nearly degenerate pair λ₂ = +0.371, λ₃ = −0.342 of opposite signs (|λ₃/λ₂| = 0.922): their contributions interfere sign-alternatingly at low m before the slower λ₂ layer comes to the fore. For d = 4 the pair λ₂ = +0.292, λ₃ = −0.256 is also of opposite signs, but less degenerate (|λ₃/λ₂| = 0.876), and after the anchor-constant correction (§5.4) the ratios are smooth there; the work does not give a complete quantitative explanation of the difference in interference strength between d = 4 and d = 6. **For d = 6 no analogous ε-diagnostic of the anchor constant a_F was performed:** the medians 2.556/2.830 in T5.2 are given without correction — the status explicitly differs from d = 4, where the correction is made and documented (T5.3).

Remark on the circularity of the m range: the primary source states the valid range m = 7…13 for the ratio §5; m = 14…17 carries an artefact of the Richardson recursion (a_F is built from the same levels) and is not included in the fit (§5.4). The number 3.585 is not the median over 13 zeros, as formulated earlier, but the specific ratio δ_{m+1}/δ_m at m = 12; an independent reimplementation gives close pilot values (2.40, 4.91; geometric mean 3.0150, 4.4337) on other pairs of levels — both series agree with the same λ₂ layer within the expected spread over m.

The discrepancy between the originally stated accuracy of the anchor constant a_F(4) (4·10⁻²³) and the actual diagnostic ε-correction (≈8.6·10⁻²²) — by about a factor of 20 — is not explained by the source of this work. Practical consequence: **after the ε-correction the law gives no point prediction for d = 4; the bracket 3.36–3.46 is a consequence of the uncertainty of the very method of correcting the constant, not an internal error of the law min(δ, 1/|λ₂|)**, which here is verified only at the level of order and sign of the ratio.

**External estimate of δ₄.** Burbanks, Osbaldestin, Thurlby (2021) give the interval δ₄ ∈ [7.28468621706, 7.28468621709] (conditionally RIGOROUS anchor); a_F(4) has no separate external certification.

Mechanical interpretation of the two-term model (a candidate theorem not proved in this work, §7.3): the term C·δ^(−m) is a generic second-order correction of convergence along the unstable direction, not requiring a specific sub-leading spectrum; the term D·λ₂^m is the direct contribution of the sub-leading significant mode of the linearization. The crossover between classes: at d = 2 the δ term is slower (8.087 > 4.669, dominates), at d = 4, 6 the opposite (7.285 > 3.4266; 9.296 > 2.6949) — hence the notation rate = min(δ, 1/|λ₂|). At d = 2, 4 in the fits λ₂ enters with the value numerically verified on the discretization (d = 4) or for the true operator (d = 2); at d = 6 the operator NOT RIGOROUS data of §6 are used, and the certificate for N = 48 is an independent estimate of the same value for control.

## S9. Operator grid — details (relates to §6.2)

A special operator grid of 45 points over d ∈ [1.9, 7] (double truncation N = 40/48, control N = 56, functional residuals ≤ 10⁻²⁷) gives the following picture. **There is no smooth zero of λ₂(d):** both sub-leading branches B⁺, B⁻ are sign-constant on the whole [1.9, 7], they never intersect. The sign change of the classical λ₂ is a jump exactly at d = 2 (§2.4): for any fractional d close to 2 both branches are essential and λ₂(d) = B⁺(d) > 0 (for example, +0.15865 at d = 1.99, +0.15973 at d = 2.001), and the deviation of B⁺ from the gauge value α^(−d) decays linearly in (d − 2) on both sides (slope ≈ 0.114 per unit of d) — the branch B⁺ is smooth and passes THROUGH the gauge value, coinciding with it exactly (to 1.6×10⁻³⁰) exactly at d = 2.

**Crossover d\*.** The point at which the law changes its dominant term is defined by the condition δ(d)·λ₂(d) = 1 on the branch B⁺: bisection gives **d\* = 2.3616 ± 0.0008** (δ = 5.2155, λ₂ = 0.19175 at this point). Caveat on precision: ±0.0008 is the precision of finding the root on the grid of §6, both components NOT RIGOROUS; the crossover as a prediction of the law is independently checked only by the dynamics at d = 2.75 and 3.0 (below), not at the point d\* itself.

**Dynamical confirmation.** A direct numerical continuation of the cascade (without computing the spectrum, by directly measuring the rate of decay of corrections along the orbit) up to m = 16 at d = 2.75 and d = 3.0: the decay rate is monotone (no sign alternations in the reliable m window) with values 4.09–4.40 (d = 2.75, R² = 0.97–0.99) and 4.14–4.49 (d = 3.0), which agrees with the prediction of the branch B⁺ (4.53 and 4.22, error 2–10 %) and excludes the alternative interpretation (sign alternation, rates 5.50 and 5.04, error 11–26 %). This test played a decisive role in determining which branch enters the law at fractional d.
